Reports four randomized web-survey experiments within a sample of roughly 1,800 U.S. panel participants, comparing standardized questions with LLM-enabled confirmation and elaboration/relevance probes. Human-coded and textual outcomes indicate that probing yields more detailed and informative open-ended responses, while live coding performs moderately well but shows false-positive errors consistent with acquiescence; respondent experience declines slightly. This is a high-priority meta-analytic study because it provides randomized comparisons, large condition sizes, multiple human-coded outcomes, attrition, and respondent-experience measures.

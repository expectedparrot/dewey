# Detailed notes

## Design and findings

Pre/post training study in which 30 university students in child welfare, social work, or psychology completed four simulated investigative interviews with a fine-tuned child-avatar chatbot. Twelve received direct automated feedback on question type and 18 did not; all also received a video lecture. The model's question-type classifications agreed substantially with human coders (reported Cohen's kappa = 0.80). The feedback group improved its use of recommended open-ended questions more than the no-feedback group across subsequent sessions.

## Assessment

Relevant adjacent evidence because AI plays the interviewee and feedback coach rather than the interviewer. It demonstrates how interview-quality rules can be encoded and evaluated, but it should not be pooled with studies of AI agents eliciting data from human respondents. The small, nonrandom groups and common lecture/training exposure limit causal attribution beyond the incremental feedback comparison; transfer to real investigative interviews remains unestablished here.

Full text: *Frontiers in Psychology* 14:1198235, especially Methods and Results.

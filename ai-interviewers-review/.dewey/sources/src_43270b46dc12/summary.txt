Reports a preregistered longitudinal comparison of a messaging-platform chatbot and a conventional web survey among 304 Dutch participants over 14 days. It finds no general chatbot advantage: web surveys often had more favorable response characteristics and data quality, chatbot users supplied fewer words over time, and respondents rated the chatbot lower on enjoyment, usefulness, and security, though attention-check performance favored the chatbot. This is an important counterweight to positive chatbot studies and highlights modality, duration, privacy perception, and metric choice as moderators.

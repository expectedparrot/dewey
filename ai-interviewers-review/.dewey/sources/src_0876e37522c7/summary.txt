Evaluates three interviewing chatbots, including two LLM-based systems and a hard-coded baseline, in a study of 399 participants. The systems score well on conventional engagement and communication measures, but seldom elicit the specific motives and personalized examples associated with qualitative richness; human and LLM ratings of quality and richness also show low agreement. This is an important cautionary counterpoint for the review because it questions whether scalable conversational collection automatically produces genuinely rich qualitative evidence.
